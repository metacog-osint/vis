How likely the next seven days’ weather is to degrade operations at a reference airfield or port in each conflict zone, from up to 82 ensemble members (NOAA GEFS 31, ECMWF IFS 51), and tonight’s light.

These are forecasts with indicative thresholds, not an official forecast and not for flight planning. How Vis reads them.

Terms on this page
Ensemble members
One model run many times from slightly different starting conditions. The share of members that reach a threshold is the percentage shown.
GEFS, ECMWF IFS
NOAA’s Global Ensemble Forecast System (31 members) and the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts’ Integrated Forecasting System ensemble (51 members).
00Z, 12Z, UTC
Times in Coordinated Universal Time (Z, “Zulu”). Each day column is a UTC day.
ICD 203 term
The fixed likelihood words of the US intelligence community’s analytic standards (Intelligence Community Directive 203), such as “very unlikely” for 5 to 20%.
Green, Yellow, Amber, Red
The UK Met Office’s likelihood-by-impact colours: no significant impact, be aware, be prepared, take action.
ICAO code
The four-letter airport code from the International Civil Aviation Organization, as in UKBB for Kyiv Boryspil.
METAR
An airfield’s routine weather observation report.
ISR, UAS, VFR
Intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance; uncrewed aircraft systems (drones); visual flight rules, flying by sight rather than instruments.
JP 3-59
US joint doctrine publication 3-59, Meteorological and Oceanographic Operations.
DRC, M23, NATO
The Democratic Republic of the Congo; the March 23 Movement, an armed group in its east; the North Atlantic Treaty Organization.
Civil dusk and dawn
When the sun is 6° below the horizon, the end and start of usable daylight.

Hazard thresholds

Each day column is a UTC day. Times show in your own time zone (the Time button changes it).

Both models step every 3 hours (Open-Meteo serves the hours between interpolated), so a hazard must hold on two consecutive 3-hourly steps in a UTC day; a brief shower or gust does not colour the whole day.

    How to read the table

    • Reference points: each zone is forecast at a named airfield or port (its ICAO code is shown), not at the zone’s geographic centre, which can sit on a mountain range or in empty desert. Most zones also have an alternate airfield or port, forecast as a row of its own. The codes also let each forecast be checked against the station’s METAR reports (see “Measured skill” below).
    • Likelihood and colour: a percentage is the share of ensemble members that reach a threshold, each model weighted equally (its own share is shown beside it), given with its ICD 203 term. Most hazards use both models; visibility uses GEFS’s 31 and low cloud ECMWF’s 51, because each model publishes only one of them. The low ceiling trial uses GEFS’s 31, read from NOAA’s own files on AWS Open Data, since neither ensemble on Open-Meteo carries cloud base. Colours follow the UK Met Office likelihood-by-impact matrix.
    • What sets a cell’s colour: each hazard has an amber and a more severe red threshold, and the colour comes from the chance of reaching either one. Yellow: at least 20% for the amber threshold, or at least 5% for the red one. Amber: at least 45% for amber, or 20% for red. Red: at least 45% for red. A severe outcome therefore colours a cell at a low chance, and the line names the threshold behind the colour. Example: “Low cloud cover ≥ 95 %: Very unlikely · 6% (severe threshold)” is Yellow, because 6% is above the 5% needed for the severe threshold. The day’s colour is its worst hazard. Low cloud cover, and the low ceiling while it is on trial, never go above Yellow.
    • Tonight’s light: sunset, civil dusk and dawn and the next sunrise, the moon’s phase and lit fraction at local midnight, and low-light dark: the time with the sun more than 12° below the horizon and the moon down or under a quarter lit. Each time shows the point’s local time, then yours.
    • A point’s detail: select its name to open its latest METAR and TAF, a deterministic forecast for the next 7 days beside the past 7, today against its 1991–2020 normal and the 1991–2025 records for the date, sun and moon for the week, its elevation and Köppen climate class. The detail forecast is a single best-estimate model run and can differ from the ensemble colours, which stay the product.

    About Vis

    What it is. A program of The Intelligence Company. For each of Watchfloor’s conflict zones, Vis estimates how likely the coming week’s weather is to degrade air, ISR, maritime or ground operations, names the hazard behind it, and gives tonight’s light.

    Method. Twice a day, after the 00Z and 12Z ensemble runs reach Open-Meteo, Vis reads every member’s forecast at each point (3-hourly model steps) and counts the members that reach each hazard’s amber and red thresholds. A model whose newest run on Open-Meteo is more than 24 hours old is left out, and the page says so. Today’s column counts only the hours still ahead. Sun and moon are computed with Meeus’s formulas; checked against the US Naval Observatory at four points on 31 Oct 2026, sun times agreed within 1 minute and moon times within 2.

    Limits.

    • Not an official forecast, and not for flight planning or go/no-go decisions. Use your weather unit or the national meteorological service.
    • Thresholds are indicative. Joint doctrine (JP 3-59) names the parameters that matter but publishes no numeric limits, and real limits depend on the platform.
    • Each point is read from a model grid of about 25 km, so local effects such as valley fog, sea breezes and single thunderstorms are not resolved. There is no thunderstorm hazard yet.
    • Visibility comes from GEFS alone, where it is a diagnosed field that often runs too low in fog and rain.
    • Only wind, visibility, heat and cold can be checked against METARs, and only at stations that report; the sample since 19 Nov 2026 is small, and “Measured skill” says where it is too small to read.

    Sources

    • Ensemble forecasts: Open-Meteo Ensemble API, CC BY 4.0, free for non-commercial use.
    • NOAA NCEP GEFS 0.25°, 31 members: US public domain.
    • ECMWF IFS ensemble 0.25°, 51 members: ECMWF open data, CC BY 4.0.
    • Observations: METARs from the NOAA Aviation Weather Center, US public domain, kept in Vis’s own store: they score the forecasts, and each point’s detail shows the newest one with its age. TAFs from the same service for each point’s detail, while they are valid.
    • Sun and moon: computed by Vis from Jean Meeus, Astronomical Algorithms.
    • Point detail forecast: Open-Meteo Forecast API (best-match national and global models), CC BY 4.0, free for non-commercial use.
    • Climatology: ERA5 reanalysis through Open-Meteo’s Historical Weather API, CC BY 4.0; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information 2026; neither the European Commission nor ECMWF is responsible for any use that may be made of the Copernicus information or data it contains. Normals 1991–2020; records 1991–2025, for the 25 km grid cell, not the station.
    • Elevation: Copernicus DEM GLO-90 through Open-Meteo’s Elevation API.
    • Zones: Watchfloor’s conflict zones. Reference points: chosen by Vis (list).
